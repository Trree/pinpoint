# Phase 6: Synthesis

Load this file only after the user approves the phase 1 research brief and asks to continue.

Do not use this file during phase 1.

## Goal

Turn the earlier phases into a disciplined conclusion.

This phase is where the work becomes a clear judgment, while still preserving uncertainty and disagreement where they matter.

## Preconditions

Only proceed if the user already approved:

- a clear research question
- explicit boundaries
- a usable framework
- an evidence base
- a pressure-tested working view or counterargument record
- a scenario set when future uncertainty is material

If those are incomplete, return to the missing phase instead of synthesizing too early.

## What This Phase Should Produce

Produce a final synthesis that makes the current state of knowledge usable:

- the best current answer to the research question
- the reasoning that most strongly supports that answer
- the strongest limits on confidence
- the most important unresolved uncertainties
- the indicators or developments that would most likely change the conclusion

Do not pretend the conclusion is cleaner than the underlying work.

## Workflow

Run these steps in order:

1. Restate the approved research question.
2. Restate the current working view in its strongest defensible form.
3. Summarize the strongest supporting evidence from the ledger.
4. Summarize the strongest counterarguments, invalidation conditions, or competing interpretations.
5. If scenarios were developed, explain which scenarios are most decision-relevant and why.
6. State the current best conclusion in direct language.
7. State the confidence level and why:
   - `High`
   - `Medium`
   - `Low`
8. Identify the biggest sources of uncertainty still remaining.
9. Identify the specific observations, indicators, or future evidence that would most likely change the conclusion.
10. Stop unless the user explicitly asks for a recommendation, memo, article, or implementation plan.

## Output Shape

Use this compact backbone:

- `Research Question`
- `Best Current Answer`
- `Why This Answer Currently Holds`
- `Confidence Level`
- `Main Uncertainties`
- `What Could Change The Conclusion`
- `Stop / Continue`

Add these sections only when useful:

- `Supporting Evidence`
- `Strongest Counterarguments`
- `Most Decision-Relevant Scenario`
- `Practical Implication`

## Confidence Standards

Confidence is not a mood. It must come from the work.

Use higher confidence when:

- evidence is strong and consistent
- counterarguments have been tested and mostly contained
- invalidation conditions are clear and currently unsupported

Use lower confidence when:

- evidence is mixed or thin
- key assumptions remain weak
- plausible alternative interpretations remain unresolved
- the result depends heavily on fast-moving external conditions

## Quality Bar

Good synthesis:

- answers the question directly
- reflects the strongest evidence, not the most convenient story
- includes the strongest unresolved objections
- makes clear what would change the answer

Weak synthesis:

- repeats the research process instead of concluding
- hides uncertainty behind vague wording
- ignores the strongest counterarguments
- turns a scenario exercise into a false single-point certainty

## Stop Rule

After producing the synthesis, stop.

Do not automatically proceed into:

- policy recommendation
- investment recommendation
- writing a full article
- implementation planning

Allow at most a one-line next step, such as:

`Next step: convert this synthesis into a short decision memo.`
