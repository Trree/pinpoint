# Output Examples

Use these examples to preserve the intended shape of the skill:

- phase 1 makes the problem researchable, then stops
- phase 2 builds a framework, then stops
- phase 3 builds an evidence ledger, then stops
- phase 4 pressure-tests the working view, then stops
- phase 5 builds a scenario set, then stops
- phase 6 produces a disciplined conclusion, then stops

## Example 1

Original question:

`Will AI change education?`

Better phase 1 shape:

- identify that the scope is too broad
- narrow by time horizon, education segment, and success criteria
- offer 2-3 candidate versions
- recommend one version
- stop before evidence gathering

## Example 2

Original question:

`Should we think stablecoins will reshape global finance?`

Better phase 1 shape:

- separate payment infrastructure, banking impact, and regulation into different directions
- define time horizon and geography
- recommend a version that is concrete enough to research
- stop before building a source list

## Example 3

Original question:

`Compare React and Vue for a dashboard rewrite over the next 18 months.`

Possible phase 1 outcome:

- state that the question may already be researchable
- lightly tighten evaluation criteria if needed
- avoid forcing multiple rewrites
- stop with a readiness judgment or a minimal brief

## Example 4

Approved research question:

`Over the next 3 years, where is AI most likely to create measurable workflow gains in K-12 teaching without reducing teacher control?`

Good phase 2 shape:

- choose a lens such as `Mechanism` or `Comparison`
- break the question into 3-6 real subquestions
- identify variables such as subject type, teacher workflow, verification burden, and school constraints
- specify evidence types needed for each subquestion
- stop before collecting studies or building a source list

## Example 5

Approved subquestion:

`Which teaching workflows show measurable AI-assisted time savings without reducing output quality?`

Good phase 3 shape:

- gather a limited set of relevant evidence items
- group them by the subquestion they inform
- distinguish direct measurements from expert interpretation
- mark evidence as strong, mixed, weak, or missing
- stop before writing the final conclusion

## Example 6

Current working view:

`AI is most likely to improve teacher productivity in planning and low-stakes feedback workflows before it improves live classroom instruction.`

Good phase 4 shape:

- identify the strongest alternative view rather than a weak objection
- test whether the conclusion depends on fragile assumptions such as verification cost or teacher adoption
- name what evidence would invalidate the current view
- surface counterexamples without instantly collapsing the whole thesis
- stop before moving into future scenarios

## Example 7

Key uncertainty:

`Will AI verification costs fall fast enough for teachers to trust AI-assisted feedback at scale?`

Good phase 5 shape:

- define a baseline, upside, and downside scenario with different trigger conditions
- make each scenario mechanism-distinct rather than just more or less growth
- identify first-order effects on teacher workflow and second-order effects on school operations
- include indicators that would show which path is unfolding
- stop before collapsing the scenarios into a single final forecast

## Example 8

Research question:

`Over the next 3 years, where is AI most likely to create measurable workflow gains in K-12 teaching without reducing teacher control?`

Good phase 6 shape:

- answer the question directly in its strongest defensible form
- tie the answer to the strongest supporting evidence rather than all evidence equally
- state the main confidence limits and unresolved objections
- identify what future evidence would most likely change the judgment
- stop before turning the synthesis into a recommendation memo or article
